🔬 Technical Root Cause Analysis

The discrepancy between the realtime viewport dome and the baked offline atmospheric renderer stems from three fundamental math and physics defects in the preview compute shader Engine/SlateVulkan/Device/AtmospherePresentationSurface/Shader/DynamicSkySurface.slang:

1. Unphysical Ozone Extinction & Wavelength Hardcoding

In DynamicSkySurface.slang, ozone absorption is computed using a flat scalar airmass formula: float3 ozone = exp(-Sky.OzoneDensity * solarMass * float3(0.06f, 0.13f, 0.035f));

Because the green channel coefficient (0.13) is more than double red and blue, as the sun nears the horizon (high solarMass), green light is heavily extinguished across the whole dome. Green suppression leaves Red + Blue = Purple/Violet tint.

2. Flat Planar Airmass vs. Spherical Planet Geometry

The realtime shader calculates path optical length using 1.0 / max(0.08, view.y + 0.13). This flat plane approximation treats the planet as a flat disk rather than a sphere of radius $R_{planet} = 6,371\, ext{km}$.

It forces excessive scattering mass at all angles, causing noon to look like cloudy dusk or misty fog, whereas the baked integrator uses spherical ray-marching (ProjectMarchDistance) to calculate exact curved path distances.

3. Direct Channel Tone Mapping Clamping

The realtime shader applies radiance = radiance / (1.0 + radiance); directly inside the compute pass before converting to sRGB.

This clamps high-dynamic-range solar luminance and prevents the intense warm scattering halo around the solar disc from bleeding naturally into the surrounding atmosphere, muting the warm golden glow into a muddy magenta gradient.
